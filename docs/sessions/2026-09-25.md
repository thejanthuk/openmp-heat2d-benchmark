## `docs/reading-order.md` — an order for the two reference books

Krit asked for a reading order for Chapra, ranked by general importance first and project relevance

Chapra, in order: Ch. 4.1–4.3 (Taylor series, total numerical error), Ch. 3.4 (round-off and machine

task is to show they are the same statement.

## Krit judged *Using OpenMP* (2007) too dated — checked, and he is right

He said he did not intend to stay at that level, since the goal is senior parallel programming rather
than this project alone. Claude verified against the file:

- **`collapse` appears only as a proposal for a future release** — Ch. 9, pp. 312–313, Figure 9.2. It
  arrived in OpenMP 3.0 (2008).
- **`target`, `teams` and `distribute` are absent entirely.** They arrived in OpenMP 4.0 (2013).

So the book is OpenMP 2.5 and predates both constructs this project is graded on. What it is still
authoritative on: Ch. 5 (sequential performance and measurement) and Ch. 7–8 (races, and what the
compiler generates). The rest is superseded.

**The fix already existed:** the same publisher issued a sequel, *Using OpenMP — The Next Step* (2017),
covering affinity, accelerators, tasking and SIMD.

## `docs/book-recommendations.md` — researched, then revised once

Written after web research (editions verified, sources listed in the file). Revision 1 was then held to
the standard "is this what a senior practitioner reads?" and failed four ways, all recorded in the file:
it was mostly course textbooks; it had **no accelerator or TPU track**, the thing actually asked for; no
**measurement methodology**, although this project's worst failures were measurement failures; and
nothing on **memory consistency or coherence**. A fifth gap was performance portability.

Revision 2 added the 2020 coherence/consistency primer (open access, with a GPU chapter), Aamodt on GPU
microarchitecture, Sze & Emer on DNN accelerators, DeepMind's *How to Scale Your Model* for TPUs,
Jain/Lilja on experiment design, Kokkos and SYCL, Allen & Kennedy on loop transformations, and a papers
canon (Roofline, ECM, the TPU ISCA papers, Amdahl **and** Gustafson). Every entry is tagged **[model]**
(read once) or **[reference]** (shelf), because reading a reference book cover to cover wastes months.

## Months 1–3 sources downloaded, and a chapter-level order built from their own contents

Krit downloaded the months 1–3 material into `docs/` (gitignored — copyrighted and large) and marks what
he has with `[X]` or strikethrough in `docs/book-recommendations.md`. Claude opened each PDF and built a
six-step order, appended to `docs/reading-order.md`, driven by the next milestone and the open questions
rather than by the books' chapter order:

| Step | Source | Why this position |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Roofline paper (13 pp.) | names the vocabulary already in use here |
| 1 | Bakhvalov Ch. 2, App. A, Ch. 4 (~45 pp.) | the audit's lesson in book form; fastest payback |
| 2 | Deakin & Mattson Ch. 1–5 (~120 pp.) | the GPU Common Core = versions 3 and 4 |
| 3 | van der Pas §2.1.3 + Ch. 5 SIMD (~35 pp.) | explains the measured 1.42x/1.46x `collapse(2)` cost |
| 4 | van der Pas Ch. 4 Thread Affinity (~85 pp.) | material for the 8-threads-slowest anomaly |
| 5 | Deakin & Mattson Ch. 6 (~25 pp.) | what the standard promises about `num_teams`/`thread_limit` |
| 6 | van der Pas Ch. 6 + OpenMP 6.0 Examples | reference only, for map semantics and refcounting |

Two useful specifics found in the files: **§4.4.4 "Pointer Swapping" (p. 91)** of Deakin & Mattson is the
exact double-buffer-plus-`target data` pattern this solver uses, and **§6.2.1 "Controlling the Number of
Teams and Threads" (p. 132)** is where the standard's promise can be compared with GCC's 20-thread cap —
which is how the finding should be phrased: a GCC runtime limitation, not an OpenMP one.

Also recorded: in Bakhvalov's PDF the page numbers match the printed pages, but in the two MIT Press
scans the offset **drifts** (+37 to +45, and +18 to +21), so section titles must be searched rather than
pages jumped to. And `docs/` holds two Bakhvalov copies — the 2nd edition file is the one to read.
